Round 21 · Our paper, standards and the template builder

This round decides how Our paper (the templates, the shelves and Our standards) and the template builder look on the desk. Each option draws both; use the small tabs under "‹ The counter", or the S key, to see the other. The builder is shown on a reseller agreement Maria is building from scratch.

Everything stays: the three tabs, the one door to a new standard, the standards every contract is checked against and the playbook that learns, and in the builder Jambo proposing sections (never adding them himself), the playbook check and the blanks.

The three ideas

OptionThe ideaWhen it winsWhat it costs
Paper firstYou always see the paper. In Our paper the chosen template shows its own pages, with each of Our standards marked where it is met and where contracts came back changed. In the builder the paper fills the desk, Jambo's proposals sit on it in place, and the outline is a tab of his panel.When people judge a template by reading it, and want to see the standards where they apply.The list of templates becomes a narrow index. Marks on the paper are new to Our paper and need care so they never look like wording.
BlocksEverything as blocks you can see and move. Templates are cards on their shelf; Our standards are cards saying what we ask and what we accept, with the playbook's suggestion inside the card. In the builder the sections are the work surface: drag, add, write; the paper reads beside them.When building and reordering a new template is the main job, and for people who think in structure first.Cards show fewer templates per screen than a list. The builder's paper preview is narrow, so long clauses are read at a smaller size.
The SAP wayOur paper is a list report: tabs and labelled filters in the band, a quiet table with "Our standards met" and "Came back changed". The builder has a header that stays one line (with "Show details" to open it), an outline column, the document, and Jambo's panel with the playbook check.When templates are managed as a library, and the builder should read like every other object page.The least new. The outline takes a column away from the paper on the iPad.

My recommendation: The SAP way, with Paper first's marks

Our paper is a library: a table with "Our standards met" and "Came back changed" answers "which template should I trust?" faster than cards or an index. In the builder, the one-line header keeps the paper exactly where it is today, the outline column is always visible, and Jambo's panel holds the playbook check beside the wording.

From Paper first I would borrow the standard marks on the paper ("Payment within 30 days" ticked under its clause), shown in the builder only, so the check is beside the wording it is about.

What the checks changed

The contract's pixels. The builder's header is one line in all three options, so the document's first line sits where it does today. Measured from the top of HaTi's bar: today 138 px at 1440 and 1536, 144 px on the iPad. The options: 137 px in all three options, at 1440, at 1536 and on the iPad, so the wording sits 1 px higher than today on the laptops and 7 px higher on the iPad. To keep it one line below 1400 wide, the Category and Stream boxes drop their labels (the values stay); the SAP way keeps them in "Show details".

One door. New standard contract stays the one way to start a template, with its three starts. Draft from it opens the one New agreement form.

Copilot prepares, a person decides. Jambo's proposed sections are never added without a press on Add. Writing a section's wording with Jambo says its cost first; adding our standard wording costs nothing.

Fit. At 1536 × 730 the head card and Jambo's panel stay put; the template's paper and Our paper's lists scroll inside the desk, as a document and a list do today.

Questions for you

Marks on the paper (Paper first, and borrowed in my recommendation): small tags under a clause saying it meets one of Our standards, or that contracts came back changed there. They are not a band, but they are new words on the paper. Do you want them?
