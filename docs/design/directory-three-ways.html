The Directory, three ways
Keys 1 · 2 · 3 or ← → switch. Press a row to see its panel.

The three, side by side

OptionThe ideaWhen it winsCost
The SAP Way One quiet list of every person HaTi deals with, ours and theirs. Tabs for the side, filters beside them (party, role, stream), and the side panel shows the person's contracts and the doors onto them. Every day. "Who signs for Naivas?", "What is her address?", "Which contracts is he named on?" One search, one press. The same page grammar as Contracts, Parties and People, so little new to learn or build. Needs one reading on the server (scoped to the streams you may open) and the doors wired.
Shelves The same list, grouped under each party. Our side is the first shelf. A shelf opens to its people; pressing the shelf itself shows the party's own panel. When you think in companies first: "Who do we know at Safaricom?" and you want them all in one glance. Parties now appear on two pages (Parties and here). Long shelves need folding and the fold state must be kept on refresh. Two levels of selection in one list.
Faces A wall of contact cards with an A to Z edge, like a phone's contacts. A pressed card shows the same panel. A small book, under about sixty people, or when you pick a person by name at a glance. Fewer people on a screen and no columns to run your eye down. A new control (the A to Z edge) outside HaTi's list grammar, where every list is a table with a panel.

Recommendation: The SAP Way

  • It is the grammar every list already wears. Contracts, Negotiations, Parties, People and Requests all read this way, so nobody learns a new page and the build reuses the list and panel that exist.
  • Parties already answers "by company". The Directory should answer "by person". The two meet through the Party filter here and a "People" door on the Parties panel, so there is one list of parties and one list of people.
  • The panel is the inspector. Its doors, its facts grid and keeping your place on refresh come for free.

If you prefer Shelves or Faces, say the name and only that one is built.

What all three share

What the page reads

Nothing new is stored. The Directory is a reading, as Parties is, from three places HaTi already keeps:

  • The roster (Settings → People): your colleagues, their job titles and their role in HaTi.
  • Every contract's own people list (the one address book): the other side's signers, negotiators, advisers and readers, with the role each holds on each contract.
  • The imported contact list (Settings → Signer directory): people an admin loaded from a file. They show under "Not on a contract" until a contract names them.

One person is one email address, across contracts. The same person named on four contracts is one row with four contracts behind it. The columns are facts the record already holds: party, role, how many contracts, when they were last on one, and the address. "Last on" is the date of the newest contract activity naming them.

Where it sits

It takes over the People door in the rail, renamed Directory. People becomes its "Our people" tab, so there is one door, not two. The Parties panel gains a "People (n)" door that lands here with that party already chosen in the filter. If you would rather see it under Work beside Parties, say so; nothing else changes.

Where you end up

  • Open party uses the Parties page's own door: the Contracts page narrowed to that party, with the × chip to widen.
  • A contract in the panel opens Home's Document for that contract. The rail is the way back.
  • Draft new agreement is the existing form with the party and the person's address already filled.
  • Email is the one press that leaves HaTi, as on People today.
  • A refresh keeps the tab, the filters and the person you chose.

What it does not do

  • It writes nothing. A name or address is corrected on the contract, where the one writer lives, and every page follows. The panel's contract rows take you there.
  • Adding people stays where it is. A colleague is added in Settings → People. An outside person is added on a contract. A list is imported in Settings → Signer directory.
  • The phone is not in this round. The phone keeps its People screen for now, as Parties is not on the phone yet either.
  • No Jambo on the page. Later, his page context can carry the same reading so "Who signs for Naivas?" is answered from it. Not drawn here.

What the six questions changed

  • The standard answer. The general practice in contract software is a business-partner book: organisations with their contact persons, each with roles, and the related contracts beside them. HaTi follows it in one departure: the book is a reading off the contracts, nothing filed, because the address already has a home on the contract. The Parties page took the same decision.
  • The cheapest channel. The counts live in the band's facts line and the tabs. "Not on a contract" is a tab, not a notice. No band, strip or tip anywhere.
  • The one door. The Directory grows out of the People door instead of adding a rail door. The Parties panel gets one door into it rather than a people list of its own. Open party reuses the Parties door, Draft new agreement reuses its prefill, and correcting a person stays on the contract.
  • Where the reader ends up. The number on the Parties panel's People door is the number of rows behind it. Every door above lands on an existing page, and the rail brings you back.
  • The contract's pixels and Jambo's place do not bite: this is not a contract page and no model is called.

The page checklist

The SAP Way
  • Most important thing easiest to see: the name and the party, first two columns. Yes.
  • Related things grouped: a person's contracts and the others at their party sit in the panel. Yes.
  • Each label says what is behind it: Party, Role, Contracts, Last on, Email. Yes.
  • Know where you are: the rail door is lit, the band names the page. Yes.
Shelves
  • Most important thing: the party leads, the person comes second. Yes, if the company is what you look for.
  • Related things grouped: people under their party on the page itself. Yes.
  • Labels: a shelf row and a person row share one head. The cost: the Role column is empty on a shelf row.
  • Know where you are: yes, but a folded shelf hides who is where until opened.
Faces
  • Most important thing: the name, large on each card. Yes.
  • Related things grouped: only in the panel. The cost: no sorting by a column.
  • Labels: cards have none, the reader learns the shape. The cost.
  • Know where you are: yes. The A to Z edge is a new control to learn.

Assumptions to confirm

  • "Directory" means the people HaTi deals with, inside and outside, with their parties and contracts. If you meant something else by the word, say which and this round is redrawn.
  • The sample names, parties and contracts on the screen are made up to show the shape. Nothing here is read from your workspace.
  • Order is by name, admins first on the Our people tab, as People orders today.
