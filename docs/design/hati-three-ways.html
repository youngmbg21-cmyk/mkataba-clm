One desk, one worklist. Everything that waits on you is a row in one table, with four numbers above it that are doors.

This is what serious contract software does, and what SAP's own Horizon look does: numbers first, then a worklist, and an object page for each contract with its facts in a strip and its sections in a bar. HaTi keeps its own clothes: white sheets, grey pills, one black button, the green link.

What changes

  • Home opens on your desk: four tiles (Needs you · Negotiations · Due this week · Signed), then the worklist with tabs and columns. Jambo's box stays at the panel's foot; the big box in the middle goes.
  • A contract gets an object head: its facts in one strip (party, value, ends, signer, stream, standards, approval) and a section bar (Paper · Dossier · Clauses · Redlines · Obligations · History) above the paper.
  • Jambo's panel keeps its faces; the Dossier is grouped under small heads: To do · Read for you · Links.

When it wins

Many people doing the same kinds of work every day, who want the same shape on every screen and a number to steer by.

Cost

More furniture. The object head puts the first line of wording about 90 px lower than today, and the tiles show numbers even on a quiet morning.

Asks the owner first

The contract's pixels. The object head grows the space above the wording; your rule refuses that without your yes.

The paper is the app. Nothing is boxed; every fact says which words on the paper it was read from, and the action sits at the end of its own line.

This is Harvey's and Legora's whole idea: trust comes from the citation. Home is one quiet list where each row quotes the clause it rests on. A contract open shows the paper with notes in its margin, each tied to the line it was read from. Jambo speaks in sentences and offers the next press, with its cost.

What changes

  • Home is a sheet of type: your name, Jambo's two sentences, then one list. Each row: the contract, the fact with its § and quote, one green verb. No tiles, no cards, no big box in the middle.
  • A contract shows the paper with a margin: blanks (amber), facts (grey) and risks (brown) stand beside the line they came from, each with its press.
  • Jambo's panel loses its tabs on Home and becomes his conversation; on a contract the Dossier is a list of facts, each with its source, and the spine is one line of words.
  • The foot carries a kept question across the book (Ask a column), with its lights and what one more read costs.

When it wins

Work that is reading, checking and negotiating paper, where every claim must be defensible and the paper is the hero.

Cost

Numbers and charts are a door away, not on the first screen. The margin needs width: on the iPad (1366) the paper narrows to about 560 px or the margin folds into the panel.

Asks the owner first

Jambo's two sentences on Home. They are the panel's Overview sentence moved onto the page. It is not a band, but your rule says ask before anything new says something on a page.

The deal's journey, in one picture. Six stops across the top; every deal stands at its stop; the ones waiting on you are lifted; Jambo stands at the stops with what he prepared.

Your own rule says every step starts the next. This draws it. The same road is the Dossier's spine when a contract is open, so where you are on Home and where the deal is are one drawing. Jambo's panel is his shift: what he did overnight, and what waits on you, by stop.

What changes

  • Home opens on the road: Arrive · Read · Fill · Negotiate · Sign · Live, each with its count and whose move it is; under each stop, the deals as cards. Empty stops fold to a thin strip so the busy ones get the room.
  • A contract keeps today's paper; the panel's first face is the Road: this deal's position, what is owed at this stop, what Jambo will do at the next, and what happened behind you.
  • Needs you does not go away: it is the lifted cards and the panel's "by stop" list; the bell counts the same.

When it wins

You want to see the whole flow at a glance and steer deals from stop to stop, with Jambo's work visible as the deal moves.

Cost

A book of hundreds needs the lanes to fold to your own deals first, with "N more" under each stop. Six lanes squeeze at 1366 wide, so a card there shows name and party only.

Asks the owner first

Home's first thing. On 22 October you picked one desk, one list, for Home. The road puts the picture first and the list in the panel. That is a reversal of that pick, so it waits for your yes.

My recommendation: The Legora Way

It is the one that makes HaTi feel like Harvey and Legora, because the thing those products are trusted for is the citation: every fact shows where on the paper it was read. HaTi already reads the paper on arrival and keeps the quote; this puts that reading where the person looks, on every row and in the paper's margin. It also keeps the three things you have already chosen: the black bar, Jambo's panel on the right, and one list for what needs you.

The Road is the bolder picture and the one I would borrow from later, as the Dossier's spine. The SAP Way is the safest for a large team, and the one I would not choose for HaTi, because it makes the paper smaller on the screen.

To pick, answer with the name. Only that one is built, and it comes back to you drawn-against-built at your three screens before it merges.
