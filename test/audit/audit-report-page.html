The headline

Two things to fix first

A signed contract is not as fixed as it looks. The wording itself is genuinely locked — an attempt to rewrite it was refused outright, and that is the single most important guarantee in the product. But the title, the end date and which of your companies is named as a party can all still be changed on a signed contract, and nothing objects. Worse, a signed contract can be quietly put back to “Draft” by an ordinary save — and once it is a draft, the sealed wording can be rewritten too. Two requests, and a signed agreement becomes editable.

One person can record another person’s signature. An ordinary Editor can save a contract with your Managing Director’s name, title and email recorded as having signed it in the app. The server accepts it. From that moment the false signature is locked in by the very rule that protects real ones.

Four quieter problems follow. Any contract can be filed underneath any other by anyone who can edit it — which switches off its renewal reminders and leaves no trace in its history. Everyone in the workspace, read-only Viewers included, is handed their colleagues’ signing limits. The product says a negotiation is “waiting on the other side” when what you actually have is changes you wrote and never sent. And a contract sent from a phone leaves no line in its history at all.

Simulation one

A deal, four rounds, two chairs

A logistics agreement was drafted and two changes made: payment tightened from sixty days to thirty, and a new insurance clause inserted. A share link was created and opened in a second browser with no login — the other side’s experience exactly.

The wall held completely. The raw data sent down that link was read directly rather than trusted to the screen. It contained the contract and nothing of ours: no internal notes, no colleague’s name attached to a review, no sign a review had happened at all, no team roster, no history file, no covering note.

They refused the payment change with a reason and countered on liability. Nothing reached us until they pressed Send — checked against our own copy, which still showed nothing settled. That is the promise the page makes, and it is kept.

The refusal was reopened, countered at forty-five days, accepted, and the contract signed and sealed. The history was complete, the timeline held every event, and the integrity check came back clean.

Then the executed contract was attacked. The wording held — through the product and through a direct request to the server with no button involved. The title, the end date, the named party and the status did not.

Simulation two

An amendment on a signed contract

One press, one date typed, one note — and a new draft opened on its own Document tab carrying the letterhead, the parties, and an opening line naming the agreement it amends. It was filed against its parent from the first moment; there was no point at which it sat loose.

A clause was written into it, it was signed and executed, and the payoff checked. Everything landed. The master’s live end date moved to 31 December 2029 and its card said plainly where that date came from, naming the amendment rather than quietly showing a different number. The family counted as one agreement with two documents. The renewal reminder followed. On the signed master the obligations sweep was still pressable while the two wording checks had correctly gone dead.

This journey worked end to end with nothing to report against it.

Simulation three

Three new people

An Admin, an Editor and a Viewer were created and signed in. All three were correctly challenged for the temporary password before they could do anything.

The permission model is genuinely strong, and it was tested by attacking it rather than by looking at the screen. The Editor could not promote themselves, could not lift their own signing limit, and could not widen their own stream access — three attempts, three refusals from the server. The Viewer could read; a direct write was refused outright. Typing your way to Settings as an Editor lands you on your own account page, so the page itself is the wall rather than a hidden menu item.

One thing failed: every one of them, Viewer included, was handed their colleagues’ signing limits, who checks whose work, and who oversees whom.

Confirmed — each one reproduced against a running copy

The findings, worst first

01

A signed contract can be unlocked in two saves

Critical

What happensSaving a signed contract with its status changed to “Draft” is accepted. Once it is a draft, the sealed wording can be rewritten and saved. Both requests succeeded.

Why it mattersThe one thing a contract system exists to guarantee is that an executed agreement is the agreement that was executed. This is a two-step way around it, available to anyone who can edit contracts.

Recommended fixTreat the status of a signed contract as part of what was signed — refuse any save that moves it backwards, exactly as saves that rewrite its wording already are. That protection is written and works; it is simply not applied to the status.

02

One person can record another person’s signature

Critical

What happensAn ordinary Editor saved a contract with a colleague’s name, title and email recorded as an in-app signature on that colleague’s step. The server accepted it. Nothing checks that a signature recorded as made in the app names the person actually signed in.

Why it mattersThis is the definition of a forged signature — and once recorded, the rules that protect genuine signatures protect it too.

Recommended fixTwo small changes. Check that an in-app signature’s name and email belong to the person making the request. And include each signing step’s internal identity in the check for a tampered signing route — at present it does not look at it, which is why removing it defeats the guard.

03

Any contract can be silently filed under any other

Serious

What happensA plain Editor filed one contract underneath another simply by saving it that way. No refusal, and — the sharp part — no line in the contract’s history. Two contracts were also made each other’s parent, a loop the product’s own rules forbid but nothing on the server prevents.

Why it mattersA contract filed under another stops being counted as an agreement of its own and, more seriously, its renewal reminders stop firing. A contract that quietly leaves your renewal warnings is the one you find out about after it has auto-renewed. The missing history entry is what makes it hard to notice.

Recommended fixTreat it the way moving a contract between value streams is already treated: check the change is actually being made, allow it deliberately, refuse loops, and write a line in the history saying it happened and who did it.

04

“Waiting on the other side” is said about changes you never sent

Serious

What happensWith one change written and not yet published, the product reports the next move as being with the other side.

Why it mattersThis is the same class of problem you reported personally on MK-255 in August and which was fixed for one route. This is a second route into it. Someone reading their negotiations list sees a deal sitting with the counterparty and waits — while nothing has left the building.

Recommended fixThe product already counts changes written but not sent, and already uses that count elsewhere to say exactly this. The statement of whose move it is should ask the same question before concluding the ball is with the other side.

05

Everyone can read their colleagues’ permission settings

Serious

What happensEvery signed-in person, Viewers included, receives each colleague’s signing limit, whether their work is checked, who their standing reviewer is, and who oversees them. Only the stream-access list is withheld.

Why it mattersThese are management decisions about individuals. The rulebook says they are admin-only, and the product’s own test believes they are protected — but it only checks the one field that genuinely is.

Recommended fixRemove the other four from what a non-admin is sent, the same way stream access already is, and widen the test to check the raw data rather than the screen.

06

A refused point can be buried by closing the round

Serious

What happensWhen the other side asks for something and we refuse it, the product correctly blocks signing while the disagreement is live. Pressing “Close Round” archives the refusal and clears the block — and the contract can then be signalled ready and signed.

Why it mattersIt records agreement where there is a live disagreement the other side never withdrew.

Recommended fixA refused point the asker has not withdrawn should survive the round boundary and keep blocking signature. The product already computes exactly this list; nothing currently reads it.

07

A contract sent from a phone leaves no trace in its history

Serious

What happensCreating a share link records a line in the contract’s history — but that line is written by the desktop screen, not by the server. The phone sends the link without writing it. The link exists and is listed under sharing; the history is unchanged.

Why it matters“When did we send this to them, and who sent it?” is one of the first questions asked in a dispute, and History is where you would go. A send made from a phone is not there, and nothing looks wrong.

Recommended fixWrite the line on the server when the link is created rather than on the screen that happened to create it — then every route in, including any added later, is recorded by construction.

08

A change fingerprint can be made to match two different histories

Serious

What happensEach tracked change carries a fingerprint attesting to the exact wording before and after. It is built by joining those pieces with line breaks — and contract wording contains line breaks. Two genuinely different before/after pairs were produced that give an identical fingerprint.

Why it mattersThe fingerprint is the evidence that a change record has not been altered. Where it can match two stories, it proves less than it claims. The marked-up redline the other side actually reads is not covered by it at all.

Recommended fixSeparate the pieces in a way wording cannot imitate — recording the length of each piece before it, the standard remedy — and bring the redline marks inside what the fingerprint covers.

09

Renumbering clauses on a phone is never saved, and says it was

Serious

What happensRenumbering a contract’s clauses from a phone rewrites the wording, writes a history line and captures a version — all in memory only. The screen then says the change was “recorded in History.” Nothing is saved. On the next reload the renumbering, the repointed cross-references and the history line have all gone. The desktop version of the same action saves correctly; the phone one simply omits it.

Why it mattersSilent data loss with a confirmation message on top of it — the worst combination, because the person has no reason to check. The message itself is also broken, reading “[object Object],[object Object] headings renumbered”, which is strong evidence this path has never actually been run.

Recommended fixSave after renumbering on the phone, exactly as the desktop does. One line. The broken message is a second one-line fix beside it.

10

An amendment’s opening lines say “amend” whatever it is

Minor

Creating an Annex, a Statement of Work, a Renewal or a Side Letter produces a document correctly titled but whose body reads “the parties now wish to amend it”. A statement of work does not amend a master agreement, and a renewal extends rather than amends. The other side reads this.

Recommended fixWrite the opening and closing lines per kind. Four short variants cover all seven.

11

The end-date question promises something it does not always do

Minor

The create form asks whether the document changes the end date and says that if you set one it becomes the family’s live end date. For an Annex, Statement of Work or Side Letter that is not true — the date is stored and ignored, and the reminder does not move.

Recommended fixEither hide the question for kinds that cannot move the term, or say plainly that the date is recorded but does not move the family’s.

12

Two smaller things

Minor

The obligations row on the Checks card is always green, even when obligations are overdue — unlike the other two rows, which turn amber and red. And any signed-in person, including a Viewer, can read the workspace’s total Copilot spend.

Not bugs — decisions on the record

Worth revisiting

Each of these is written down as a deliberate choice. A lawyer would want them looked at again.

An unsigned amendment already moves the agreement’s end date

Create an amendment, type a new end date, and the master’s live end date changes before anybody has signed anything. This is the one I would most want you to think about. An unsigned amendment has no legal effect. If someone reads that date and decides not to serve a renewal notice, the decision rests on a term that does not yet exist. The counter-argument is real — you are working on an extension and want the calendar to reflect it — but the honest form of that is to show both: “ends 30 June 2027; a draft amendment proposes 31 December 2029.” My recommendation is that only a signed amendment moves the live date.

Your colleagues’ names travel with every change

The rulebook says one thing about your side crosses to the counterparty: the negotiation lead’s name. The code does more — every change carries the name of the colleague who wrote it, and so do version labels and shared comments. This may be what you want; drafters are normally named. But the rulebook and the code disagree, and one of them should be corrected. Commercially, which of your people worked on which clause tells the other side where you are worried.

Signature images and email addresses cross before execution

Once anyone on your side has signed, their name, job title, email address and the picture of their signature travel to the counterparty on every link — including a negotiation link, to a party who has signed nothing. The reason for showing a signature at all is sound: they need to know the deal is waiting on them. The name and date achieve that. The email address and the image do not, and a held signature image is the raw material for forgery.

“Seal valid” is said with the same confidence over a weak seal

If a contract is sealed while the browser cannot reach proper cryptography, HaTi falls back to a much weaker fingerprint — correctly, so that sealing still works. But the verify button then reports “Seal valid” without mentioning that the seal is the weak kind. Two other screens in the product do say so. This one should too.

Wording can still move between the first signature and the last

On a contract with more than one signer, wording can change after the first person signs and before the last one does — so the first signer’s mark ends up on wording they did not see. Worth deciding whether the document should freeze at the first signature rather than the last.

If you fix nothing else, fix these in this order

Suggested fix order

  1. Stop a signed contract being put back to Draft Smallest change, largest consequence — it currently unlocks everything else.
  2. Check an in-app signature belongs to the person making it Forgery is worse than tampering, because it looks authentic.
  3. Guard the filing of one contract under another, and record it Silent loss of renewal reminders is the failure you find out about too late.
  4. Freeze the title, end date and named party on a signed contract The second half of finding one.
  5. Stop saying “waiting on them” about unsent changes Small fix, and the second time this class has bitten.
  6. Withhold colleagues’ permission settings from non-admins One change at the point the data is assembled.
  7. Keep refused points alive across a round boundary The right list is already computed and simply not read.
  8. Record a share on the server, not on the screen that sent it Cheap, and it closes the gap for every future route in as well.
  9. Save a phone renumber One line — it is silent data loss with a confirmation on top of it.
What this audit did not cover. The Copilot’s own answers and any AI-written wording; email delivery beyond confirming nothing is actually sent in testing; the phone shell screen by screen; the Swedish market pack beyond confirming the new wording exists in both languages; performance, hosting, backups and physical security. The four-round negotiation was played once, not repeatedly with varied timing. Two known pre-existing test failures were excluded by instruction and are unrelated to anything here.

Every finding above was reproduced against a running copy of HaTi before it was written down; eight further claims were attacked, held, and are not reported.
