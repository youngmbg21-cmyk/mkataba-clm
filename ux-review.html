HaTi — how it really feels to negotiate a contract in it

A user-experience review of the HaTi contract platform (repo youngmbg21-cmyk/mkataba-clm). Every claim below was checked against the actual code — the file and function names are given so you can find them. Reviewed at commit 2d4cd99, 26 July 2026. Nothing here is imagined; if a feature is missing, it says so.

The two people in the story

Wanjiru owns a small catering company in Nairobi. She drafted a 12-month supply agreement inside HaTi, using the built-in "Raw Material Supply Agreement" template (js/templates.js, template RM) and the guided wizard (js/wizard.js).

Erik is a procurement manager at a mid-size Swedish firm. In Scenario 1 he only works in Microsoft Word with tracked changes over email. In Scenario 2 he agrees to work inside HaTi.

First: what the app actually has (and doesn't)

Before the role-play, here is the honest inventory, from reading the code:

YES — Draft a contract from 12 Kenyan templates with a fill-in wizard (js/wizard.js).
YES — Share by email/WhatsApp/link; the other side opens a review page with no account needed (js/views/portal.js).
YES — The other side can propose edits; the owner sees them as a colour-marked comparison and accepts or rejects (js/versioning.js).
YES — Version history with side-by-side compare, on both sides of the deal (renderVersionsSection, openPortalVersionCompare).
YES — E-signing with a 6-digit email code, a digital seal, and a full audit trail (portalStartOtp, finalizeExecution).
YES — A Word round-trip (download the .docx, get a marked-up copy back, file it as a negotiation round) — but only for contracts that arrived as an uploaded Word file (js/wordflow.js, gate: isWordDoc()).
NO — Export a HaTi-drafted contract to Word. There is no Word writer anywhere. The code says it itself: "HaTi has no Word writer" (js/views/portal.js, line 21).
NO — Import a marked-up Word file into a HaTi-drafted contract. The upload button files it as a new, separate contract instead.
NO — Accept some of a counterparty's edits and reject others. It is the whole round or nothing (acceptProposedRound).
NO — A counterparty account or login. "Inviting" Erik really means emailing him a one-shot link. Team invites exist, but they would put Erik inside Wanjiru's company.
PARTLY — "PDF export" is the browser's print dialog (exportPDF ends in window.print()); the user must pick "Save as PDF" themselves.
PARTLY — Each share link accepts exactly one response, then goes dead. Every negotiation round needs a freshly created link.

Scenario 1 — Erik refuses HaTi and lives in Microsoft Word

Wanjiru drafted the agreement in HaTi. Erik will only exchange Word files with tracked changes, by email. Six full rounds. Watch where HaTi helps and where it simply is not in the room.

Round 1 Wanjiru sends the draft to Erik

  • She wants to email Erik an editable Word file of her draft.
  • Screen used Contract workspace (js/views/contract.js). The toolbar offers: Edit · Share · Import · Compare · PDF. There is no Word button — the Word panel (wordControlsHtml in js/wordflow.js) only renders for contracts that were uploaded as .docx, and hers was drafted inside HaTi.
  • Steps She tries PDF (2 clicks + the print dialog). Erik replies: "I can't track-change a PDF, send Word." So she selects the contract text in the browser, copies it, pastes it into a blank Word document, and rebuilds the headings, clause numbers and signature block by hand. Roughly 12 clicks plus 20–30 minutes of reformatting.
Blocked in the first five minutes. She searches the toolbar for "Export to Word", finds only PDF, and is forced outside the app before the negotiation even starts. Every later round inherits this problem.
Friction #1 (Scenario 1, Round 1). The problem area is circled in red: the toolbar in js/views/contract.js has no way to produce a Word file. The green button on the right is the proposed fix.

Round 2 Erik returns his marked-up Word file

  • She wants to load Erik's redlined .docx into HaTi and see his changes highlighted.
  • Screen used None fits. The Import button (openImportModal, js/core.js:1547) only accepts a pasted response code from a share link — not a file. The upload flow files his .docx as a brand-new, separate contract. The real Word-return feature (uploadWordVersion, js/wordflow.js:102) is unreachable, because her contract fails the isWordDoc() check.
  • Steps She reviews Erik's tracked changes in Word itself. Then, to keep HaTi truthful, she opens Edit (openEditDocModal, js/views/contract.js:1103), pastes the whole new text over the old one, and saves. About 6 clicks in HaTi plus the full review done outside it.
Three things quietly go wrong. (1) The version is recorded as "Edited by Wanjiru" — the audit trail now says she made Erik's changes. (2) The edit modal warns that the first edit "converts the drafted layout into working text" — the neat template formatting and the quick-fill fields are gone for good. (3) There is no accept/reject moment in HaTi at all; the decision already happened in Word.
Friction #2 (Scenario 1, Round 2). The red circle marks the missing door: no path from a returned Word file into a HaTi-drafted contract, even though js/wordflow.js contains exactly this feature for uploaded contracts.

Round 3 Wanjiru counters

  • She wants to send Erik her counter-changes, marked up so he can see them.
  • Screen used None. To give Erik tracked changes she must make her edits in Word, with Track Changes on. HaTi cannot write a Word file, so it cannot mark her changes into one either.
  • Steps All the real work happens in Word and email. If she wants HaTi's record to stay true, she then repeats the paste-into-Edit routine (Edit → paste → save), creating version 3. Double data entry, every round.
The quiet failure begins here. HaTi has become a diary she must keep by hand about a negotiation happening somewhere else. Most busy people stop keeping the diary. From this round on, the "version history" in HaTi only shows what she remembered to paste, not what was actually offered and refused.

Round 4 Erik replies again

  • She wants to see what changed between his last file and this one.
  • Screen used Word's own compare, outside HaTi. HaTi's Compare (openCompareModal, js/versioning.js:169) can only compare the snapshots she pasted in — and only if she pasted them.
  • Steps Review in Word; optionally paste-back #3 into HaTi.
Realistic outcome: she skips the paste-back this round. HaTi is now one version behind reality and nobody notices.

Round 5 Near-final pass

  • She wants to confirm only two clauses are still open.
  • Screen used Email threads and Word. HaTi's negotiation panel (renderNegotiationSection, js/core.js:834) shows zero rounds — every "round" of this deal happened outside it, so the feature built for exactly this is empty.
  • Steps None inside the app.

Round 6 Agreement — and a dead end at the finish line

  • She wants to get the final text signed and sealed in HaTi.
  • Screen used She pastes the final wording (Edit → save), then signs: consent tick + signature pad (signDocument, js/views/contract.js:2325). Her half works.
  • Steps / the wall Erik's signature has exactly two paths. (a) He opens a HaTi share link and signs with an email code — the tool he has refused all along; best case, one click of hypocrisy. (b) They sign on paper — but then the signed scan cannot be attached to this contract; uploading it creates a second, separate record, and the contract she nursed through six rounds never honestly reaches "Signed".
Blocked at the very last step. A deal negotiated outside HaTi cannot cleanly finish inside it unless the counterparty finally uses HaTi anyway.
Friction #3 (Scenario 1, Round 6). Red circle: a paper-signed deal cannot land on the contract it belongs to.

Scenario 1 verdict — brutally honest

2/10

The single worst friction point: for a contract drafted inside HaTi, the app can neither produce nor accept a Word file. The one feature that would carry this whole scenario — the Word round-trip in js/wordflow.js, which is genuinely well built — is locked to contracts that arrived as Word files. It exists on the wrong side of the deal.

Would a real Kenyan SME owner keep using this? No — not for any counterparty who works in Word, which in cross-border trade is most of them. Wanjiru would stop updating HaTi by round 3, finish the deal in Word and email, and next time she would draft in Word from the start. HaTi would survive, at best, as a filing cabinet for the final PDF — and even that files as a second, disconnected record. The drafting wizard, the diffs, the seal — none of it gets a chance, because the app leaves the room the moment Erik says "just send the Word file".

Scenario 2 — Erik agrees to negotiate inside HaTi

First, an honest correction to the setup: HaTi has no counterparty account to invite Erik into. "Both parties in HaTi" really means Erik uses the no-login review page (the "portal", renderSharePortal in js/views/portal.js) that opens from an emailed link. Each link accepts exactly one response, then goes dead. That design decision shapes every round below.

Access How Erik gets in

  • Wanjiru does Contract → Share → types Erik's name and email → picks link expiry → Send (openShareModal, js/core.js:1317). 6 steps.
  • Erik does Clicks the link in his email. No account, no password. The page shows the contract with all filled-in terms readable, who sent it, and when the link expires.
This part is genuinely good. Zero-friction entry for the counterparty is the right call for SME deals. (Caveat: if the server has no email key set up, the email never leaves — it sits in an admin "outbox" and Wanjiru must copy the link and send it herself. She may not discover this until Erik says "what email?")

Round 1 Erik proposes edits

  • He wants to change the delivery clause and the payment terms, and say why.
  • Screen used Portal → "Propose edits (redline)" button. The entire contract appears as plain text in one big typing box (pt-redline-text textarea, js/views/portal.js:516). He scrolls through raw text to find clause 4, edits it in place, adds one overall comment, fills in his name, submits. ~7 steps.
  • What HaTi does well His edits travel as a proper redline; nothing is lost.
The confusion moment: a procurement manager expects to comment on a clause. Instead he is handed the whole agreement as one wall of unformatted text and asked to rewrite it. It works, but it feels like editing a contract in Notepad — and one slip of the keyboard silently deletes a paragraph nobody notices until the diff.
Friction #4 (Scenario 2, Round 1). Red circle: the single textarea in js/views/portal.js that stands in for a contract editor.

Round 2 Wanjiru reviews — and hits the all-or-nothing wall

  • She wants to accept Erik's delivery change but refuse his payment change.
  • Screen used An amber "Changes returned" strip appears on the contract (returnedChangesStrip, js/views/contract.js:1822) → "Review changes" opens a full-screen colour-marked comparison (reviewProposedRound, js/versioning.js:239). Reading it is excellent. Erik's response arrived automatically — no code-pasting in server mode (pollPendingResponses).
  • The wall The buttons are Accept changes / Reject — for the whole round as one block (acceptProposedRound adopts the entire proposed text). To take one change and not the other she must accept everything and then hand-edit the unwanted part back out, or reject everything and retype the part she liked. ~10 steps either way, all in the plain-text editor.
  • Also lost here The moment she accepts a redline, the formatted document becomes plain text permanently (js/versioning.js:291 — "the counterparty edited plain text, so the document is no longer formatted"). The contract gets visibly uglier as the deal progresses.
This is the worst moment of Scenario 2. Real negotiations are never take-it-all-or-refuse-it-all. Every mixed round — which is every round — turns into manual text surgery.
Friction #5 (Scenario 2, Round 2). Red circle: the whole-round Accept/Reject pair in reviewProposedRound (js/versioning.js).

Round 3 Wanjiru sends her position back — the link treadmill starts

  • She wants to get the updated text back in front of Erik.
  • Screen used Share modal again (openShareModal). The recipient fields are blank — no memory of the person she shared with two days ago. She retypes "Erik", retypes his email, picks expiry, sends. The negotiation panel even tells her this is the routine: "After resolving, re-share the updated document to send the next round" (js/core.js:867).
  • Erik sees A new email, a new link. To his credit HaTi handles the arrival well: an amber banner — "…has revised this contract since you last opened it" — with a one-click "See what changed" comparison (portalRevisedBanner), plus a version list numbered the same way Wanjiru sees it (openPortalVersionCompare). If he opens an old link instead, it plainly says "This is an older copy — it can no longer be answered".
Six rounds = six hand-built shares. Erik's email address will be typed from scratch five or six times before this deal closes. Each retype is a chance to send a live contract to the wrong address.
Friction #6 (Scenario 2, Round 3). Red circle: the always-empty recipient fields in openShareModal (js/core.js).

Round 4 Erik counters on the now-plain text

  • He wants to tighten one clause and see what happened to his earlier asks.
  • Screen used The new link. A banner tells him the outcome of his rounds — e.g. "2 of your 3 rounds accepted" (portalRoundBanner) — which is honest and welcome. But the document he now edits is the flattened plain-text version: the headings and numbering he saw in round 1 are gone (they died in round 2's accept). The discussion — why Wanjiru refused his payment term — is not in the portal at all; her reasoning lives in the email she wrote separately. Comments only flow one way per response.
  • Steps Same textarea routine, ~7 steps.
Confusion moment: "Why does the contract look worse than the one I was first sent?" A counterparty can reasonably wonder whether this plainer document is even the same instrument.

Round 5 The money round

  • He wants to propose a lower annual value.
  • Screen used Portal → "Request changes" — this reveals a "Propose a different value" number field plus his comment. Clean. Wanjiru accepts the round (resolveRound) and the value updates on the contract — and HaTi correctly voids any internal approvals she had collected (c.approval=null), so if her approval gate applies she must get re-approved before signing. Honest, but it's another loop she didn't expect.
  • Steps Erik ~5, Wanjiru ~4 (+ re-approval if the gate is on) + reshare #5 (retype email again).

Round 6 Agreement and signing — the strong finish

  • Erik does On the final link: "Accept the wording (without signing)" — a real and thoughtful distinction (pt-accept). Then, on the signing pass: Approve & sign → draw/type/upload a signature → 6-digit code sent to his email → verified, signed (portalStartOtp → portalVerifyAndSign).
  • Wanjiru does Consent tick → sign in the workspace. When the last signature lands, HaTi freezes the exact text, seals it with a digital fingerprint, and writes the audit trail (finalizeExecution). If a signing order was set up, the app even opens the share modal for her at the right moment — though yes, with Erik's email blank again.
The ending is the best part of the product. Email-verified counterparty signature, a seal that proves which exact text was signed, an evidence pack, and version history that both sides can read with the same numbering. This is better than what most SMEs do today (a scanned signature on the fifth emailed Word file).

Scenario 2 verdict — brutally honest

5/10

The single worst friction point: the round-trip machinery. One-shot links plus a from-scratch share form plus all-or-nothing acceptance means a six-round negotiation costs six hand-built shares, five retyped email addresses, and at least two sessions of manual text surgery — while the document itself sheds its formatting along the way. The individual screens are often good; the loop between them is what wears people down.

Would a real Kenyan SME owner keep using this? A qualified yes. For the common case — one or two rounds, then sign — HaTi genuinely beats email: instant access for the other side, readable diffs, verified signing, a tamper-proof record. Wanjiru would keep it for those deals. But for a genuinely contested negotiation like this one, she would finish it grinding her teeth, and next time a deal looks heavy she'll drift back to Word and email until signature time. The product keeps her as a user, but loses the negotiation itself around round 3.

What to fix, in order

Eight items, ordered by how much pain they remove per unit of work. Sizes: small = days, medium = 1–2 weeks, large = several weeks. (The mobile/WhatsApp counterparty portal is intentionally not on this list — it is deferred by design.)

1. Let a returned Word file into every contract

small

Change: remove the "must have arrived as .docx" gate (isWordDoc in js/wordflow.js) so "Upload returned .docx" appears on drafted contracts too. The file reader (docxExtract) and the round-filing machinery already exist and already handle tracked changes.

Why it hurts today: Scenario 1 collapses at round 2 — Erik's edits must be retyped by hand and get logged under Wanjiru's name, corrupting the audit trail.

2. One-click "Send updated version" reshare

small

Change: after a round is accepted or rejected, show a button that reshares to the last recipient (name, email and channel are already stored per share in the server's shares table) — one confirm, done. Prefill the share modal from the previous share in all other cases.

Why it hurts today: every round of Scenario 2 begins with retyping the counterparty's details into a blank form; six rounds means six chances to mistype a live contract's destination — and the friction quietly discourages another round even when one is needed.

3. Export any contract as a real Word (.docx) file

medium

Change: add a "Download as Word" button to the workspace toolbar and to the portal, generating a genuine .docx (the format HaTi already knows how to read; writing a minimal one is the same ZIP-plus-XML in reverse). At minimum, offer the portal's existing "HTML saved as .doc" trick (portalDownloadCurrentAsDoc) to the owner too — today it is buried in the portal and only for uploaded files.

Why it hurts today: Scenario 1 dies in round 1: Wanjiru copy-pastes into Word and rebuilds the formatting by hand, ~30 minutes of avoidable work, before the negotiation even begins. Combined with fix #1 this makes the Word-only counterparty a fully supported loop: export → they mark up → upload the return.

4. Accept or reject each change, not the whole round

large

Change: in the redline review (reviewProposedRound), group the diff into change blocks and give each one its own accept/reject control; build the adopted text from the decisions. Rejected blocks travel back to the counterparty as still-open points.

Why it hurts today: every mixed round in Scenario 2 (and they are all mixed) forces the owner into manual text surgery in a plain-text box — the single biggest reason a contested negotiation feels safer in Word.

5. A durable negotiation link per counterparty

medium

Change: instead of a fresh one-shot link per round, give each counterparty one long-lived link per contract that always shows the current state and accepts the next response. Keep one-shot links for the final signature if the extra ceremony is wanted. (This is the same web portal with a different link lifecycle — not the deferred mobile portal.)

Why it hurts today: Erik collects six emailed links and must always find the newest; opening an old one shows "this copy can no longer be answered". The dead-link ceremony exists only because links are single-use.

6. Keep the formatting through negotiation rounds

large

Change: stop converting a formatted document to plain text when a redline is accepted (acceptProposedRound) or edited in the portal. HaTi already has a rich-document engine (js/richdoc.js, js/richpaste.js) — use it for the portal editor and for merging accepted changes, so headings, clause numbering and tables survive the deal.

Why it hurts today: the contract visibly degrades the moment negotiation starts; by round 4 the counterparty is editing a wall of plain text and may doubt it is the same document. The final signed instrument is the ugly version.

7. "File these changes on behalf of the counterparty"

small

Change: in the Edit modal (openEditDocModal), add an option: "these changes came from the counterparty (received outside HaTi)". It files the edit as a negotiation round under the counterparty's name — with the normal review/accept step — instead of a silent owner edit.

Why it hurts today: whenever the deal partly happens outside HaTi (all of Scenario 1), the audit trail records the owner as the author of the other side's changes. For a product whose selling point is an honest record, that is the record lying.

8. Show the conversation in the portal

medium

Change: carry round comments in both directions. The share payload (buildSharePayload) already sends round outcomes to the portal; add the comments — Erik's asks and Wanjiru's replies — as a small thread beside the document, so the "why" of each round lives with the contract.

Why it hurts today: the portal tells Erik that his round was rejected but not why; the reasoning happens in a parallel email thread, which is exactly the fragmentation HaTi exists to end.

A closing word

The unusual thing about this codebase is that its best engineering — the Word file reader, the tracked-change handling, the versioned file ledger, the honest audit trail — already solves most of Scenario 1's pain. It is simply switched on for the wrong case (contracts the other side drafted) and switched off for the case an SME owner lives in (contracts she drafted herself). Items 1–3 above are mostly about unlocking doors that are already built, and together they would move Scenario 1 from a 2 to roughly a 6 within a couple of weeks of work.

Method note: this review is based on reading the source code (frontend modules in js/, server in server/server.js, tests in test/) and tracing each user action through the actual functions named above. Step counts are counted from the real click paths in the code. No feature described here was assumed from what "a typical CLM tool" would have.
