# THE HANDBOOK — HaTi's own instruction book, one chapter per thing you can do

**Status: PROPOSAL, nothing built (26 Oct 2026). Waits for the owner's yes on the picks in §9.**

## 1. What the owner asked (26 Oct 2026)

"I want to create a page with a link in the guide page that shows instructions similar to the information in
this artifact [the Discover user guide]. I want the same exact instructions format and include other processes
that you deem critical. This guide page should also be in subsections so it is easy to follow. Also, there should
be a rule that whenever the platform changes or new features added, the guide instruction book is updated
accordingly."

## 2. The one idea

HaTi already has the **Guide** tab in the right-hand panel: the map of every page in the order a contract lives
(Someone asks · Draft it · Agree the words · Sign · Keep it alive · See the whole book). It says WHERE things are.
It does not say HOW to do them.

The **Handbook** is the HOW. It is one page inside HaTi, opened from one line at the foot of the Guide
("How to do each thing › Handbook"). It is divided into the same seven parts the Guide uses, so the two agree.
Every chapter is written in the exact format of the Discover guide, and every "Where to find it" path is a live
door: press it and HaTi takes you there.

And it is kept honest by a rule: a change that adds, moves, renames or removes anything a person can see or
press updates its chapter in the same change, and an automatic check fails the build when a door has no chapter
or a chapter quotes a button that no longer exists.

## 3. The chapter format — the same as the Discover guide, exactly

Every chapter has, in this order:

| Part | What it is | Example from the Discover guide |
|---|---|---|
| Eyebrow | where it lives, who can (admins) | "Settings · admins" |
| Title | the feature's name, the one HaTi uses on the screen | "Company facts" |
| What it is | one or two sentences | "Turn a question into a standing company check…" |
| **When to use it** | two to four bullets, real situations | "Before an audit, a sale of the business…" |
| **Where to find it** | the path, as chips | Contracts › Jambo's panel › Discover |
| **How** | numbered steps; a pressed thing in bold | 1. Press **Ask a column**… |
| **Reading it / What it says** | only where there is something to read (lights, a line of words) | the five dots and their meaning |
| Tip | one green box: the rule, the cost, who can | "Only admins can switch a fact on." |
| Pictures | on the right, red boxes and numbers, a caption each, press to enlarge | |
| Cost | "Free" or "1 Jambo call per …", on the chapter and in the part's table | |

Each PART opens with its own **"Which one do I need?"** table (You want to… · Use · Where · Cost), and a row of
chips listing its chapters. One table for the whole book would be fifty rows long; one per part is the
subsection the owner asked for.

Two things the in-app page can do that the artifact could not:
- **Take me there.** The path chips are live doors (through Jambo's one mover, `caGo`, the same way the Guide's
  rows move). A door the reader may not use is greyed with why (a viewer on "Ask a column", a non-admin on
  Company facts), never dead.
- **You are here.** The chapter for the page in front is marked, the way the Guide marks it.

## 5. Where it lives and how it is reached (the six questions, where they changed something)

- **The page.** A HaTi page like Settings: contents on the left (the seven parts, each opening to its chapters),
  the chapter in the middle, Jambo's panel on the right with his box at the foot. Head: the one white card
  (`.ph-card`), the bar says Handbook. Wide pictures sit to the right of the words; on a narrow screen they
  stack under. A refresh lands on the same chapter (`PLACE_PARTS`).
- **The door.** ONE line at the foot of the Guide panel, under Settings & rules: "How to do each thing ›
  Handbook". Not a band, not a tip: a row like the Guide's others (question 2, the cheapest channel).
  The ways in all end at that one page (question 5): the Guide's row; "/" and the word "handbook"; Jambo's
  existing "help" words ("help", "what can you do"), which today answer with chips and would now also offer the
  Handbook; a chapter's own link (`#handbook/3.3`) from the bell or a colleague.
- **The contract's pixels** (question 3): the Handbook is its own page; nothing on the paper moves.
- **Jambo's place** (question 4): no AI in round 1. The Handbook is read. Later (round 4) Jambo answers "how do I…"
  by landing on the chapter, through the one search (`hatiSearch`), still no AI.
- **Where the reader ends up** (question 6): ‹ in the bar goes back where they came from; "Take me there" lands
  through `caGo` and `landOn`, pressing nothing; a chapter whose door is walled says who lifts it.
- **The standard answer** (question 1): serious software ships its manual inside the product, reachable from the
  help panel, one chapter per task, with pictures and a path; SAP's in-product help works this way. We follow it.
- **The phone**: the Guide tab is not on the phone, and neither is the Handbook in round 1. Named as left out.

## 6. Pictures — taken by a script, so they can be re-taken

The pictures are the part that goes stale first. Hand-taken pictures cannot be kept honest by a rule. So:

- Every chapter names the test stage that shows its screen (the Chromium stages already seed example data) and
  the things to box (by their ids). A script, `test/chromium/handbook-shots.js`, opens the stage at 1440×780,
  takes the picture, draws the red boxes and numbers FROM THE ELEMENTS' OWN POSITIONS, and saves it under
  `handbook/img/`. The server serves that folder like `/sample-contracts`.
- When a screen changes, the maker runs the script again and the pictures follow. A chapter whose boxed element
  is gone FAILS the run: the picture cannot be taken until the chapter is rewritten. That is how the owner's rule
  reaches the pictures.
- Round 1 and 2 ship the words and the doors with a grey "picture to come" frame; round 3 ships the script and
  the pictures. (Order can be swapped if the owner prefers pictures first.)

## 7. Two languages

The Handbook is part of the product, so it is in both books, like every label (Swedish written in the same
change). Its words do NOT go into js/i18n.js (already past 300 KB): they live in their own file, `js/handbook.js`,
as data, one chapter at a time, `en` and `sv` side by side. The page builder draws them. Contract text is never
translated; the Handbook quotes none.

The path chips are not typed by hand: a chapter stores the door's KEY and the chips read the page's own name from
the dictionary (`gd_p_*`, `CA_PLACES`). Rename a page and every path in the book follows (flow rule 1: one fact,
one home).

## 8. THE RULE the owner asked for, and what enforces it

Proposed as flow rule 9d in CLAUDE.md (four lines, the house limit):

> 9d. EVERY CHANGE UPDATES THE HANDBOOK (owner, 26 Oct 2026) — a change that adds, moves, renames or removes
> anything a person can see or press, or changes what a step costs or who may do it, rewrites its chapter in
> `HANDBOOK_CHAPTERS` (js/handbook.js, both books) in the SAME change and re-takes its picture
> (`handbook-shots`); a new door gets a chapter or a line in one. f9xx fails otherwise: every `CA_PLACES` word,
> `NAV_ACTS` act and Guide row is named by a chapter; every button a chapter quotes (`ui:`) is a live label;
> `DOORS_NOT_IN_THE_HANDBOOK` only shrinks. The book's foot says the day it was last checked.

What a machine CAN check, and will (the test, like f842 for the Guide):
1. Every page and act Jambo can open (`CA_PLACES`, `NAV_PLACES`, `NAV_ACTS`, the Guide's rows) is named by at
   least one chapter. A new door with no chapter fails the build. A list of today's debt
   (`DOORS_NOT_IN_THE_HANDBOOK`) is allowed only to shrink.
2. Every button or word a chapter shows in bold as "press this" is stored as the label's KEY, not typed; a
   renamed or retired label fails the test ("the Handbook quotes a button that no longer exists").
3. Every chapter has both languages, a part, a door, and a picture stage; the picture script finds what it boxes.
4. The Handbook's "last checked" date is not older than the newest change to a screen's labels (the test
   compares it with the dictionary file's last change in git).

What a machine CANNOT check, said plainly: whether the sentences still tell the truth after a screen's
BEHAVIOUR changes. That part is the rule itself (the maker's job, in the same change), plus the house habits that
already exist: the six questions are asked before any visible change, the three screens are photographed before a
merge, and the chapter's "checked on" date is part of the merge checklist. The rule lands the house way: four
lines in CLAUDE.md, its tests under its heading in MAP-TESTS.md, the story in MAP-HISTORY.md.
