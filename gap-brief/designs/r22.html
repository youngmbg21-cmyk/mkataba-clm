Round 22 · Import

This round decides how bringing in old contracts in bulk looks on the desk: the drop area, the figures, and above all the files Jambo read but was not sure about, which wait for a person to check and file. Each option shows the same batch: 24 old contracts from the shared drive, 19 filed, 3 to check, 1 duplicate, 1 that could not be read.

Everything stays: the allowance and the cost said before a batch starts, "Import as" and "File under", the optional list whose facts win over what Jambo reads (and which lists what it could not read rather than guessing), the duplicate choices, the six marks, and nothing filed for a file that needs you until you press.

The three ideas

OptionThe ideaWhen it winsWhat it costs
One at a timeThe files that need you come first, one at a time: the file's own page on the left, with numbers where each fact was found (in its own language, never translated), and the facts to file on the right. Jambo says why he was not sure and offers the likely answer.When checking is the real work, and a person wants to see the words a fact came from before filing it.The batch as a whole is behind two tabs. A fresh batch with nothing to check needs a different first screen.
Sorted by outcomeThe batch as a report: one bar shows how the 24 files ended, then three cards: to check, to decide, filed. Each row carries its own buttons. Jambo sums up the night's work and what it cost.The morning after a big batch, when you want the whole picture before starting.Checking a file still opens it somewhere else, so it is one press further from the work. Long batches make long cards.
The SAP wayFigures across the band (each one narrows the list), the drop area with its choices, a quiet table of files with the six marks and a state, and the chosen file's details in Jambo's panel, ready to file.When batches are large and mixed, and people filter and work down a list.The file's own words are one press away (Open the file) rather than beside the facts.

My recommendation: The SAP way, with One at a time for "Check all 3"

Import is a list job most days: drop files, see the figures, work down what needs you. The SAP way does that in the same grammar as Contracts and Requests, and the figures are doors that narrow the list. Jambo's panel holds the chosen file's facts, with the uncertain one in amber.

When you press "Check all 3", it should open One at a time's view: the file's own page beside the facts, with numbers where each fact was found. That is where checking is fastest and safest.
