## COMPARE
Round 16 · Obligations and calendar

This round decides how two pages that belong together look once Jambo brings them onto the desk: the obligations worklist and the calendar. They share the same dates, the same people and the same chase. Each option applies ONE idea to both pages; press Obligations or Calendar in the strip (or the page's own tabs under “‹ The counter”) to see each.

Everything that works today stays: tabs with All first, overdue meaning late work somebody could do, unassigned drawn in grey, a chase to the other side, the review desk for what Jambo found, six weeks in the window, Monday to Friday, an empty hour that takes your own event, and Jambo planning the week. Nothing is built until you pick one by name.

The three ideas
Option	The idea	When it wins	What it costs
Whose move	Sort by who has to act. We owe (you, colleagues, nobody yet in grey) beside They owe (each with Chase). The calendar colours every date by whose it is.	When the daily question is “what is mine, and whom do I chase?”, and most work is chasing the other side.	Dates are split across two columns, so “what comes next, whoever owes it” takes two looks. Two columns need width: below about 1100 wide they stack.
Week by week	The week is the unit on both pages. Late on top, this week open day by day, later weeks folded into one line each. The calendar opens this week by the hour and keeps the other five as one row each.	When people plan by the week, and want the list and the calendar to read the same way.	A folded week hides its rows until pressed (its line says what is inside). The calendar shows hours only for one week at a time.
The SAP way	A white band with labelled filter boxes and a saved-view menu, a quiet table with acts above it, and the selected row's details in the side panel. The calendar is a planning calendar: one row per contract, six weeks of weekdays across.	When people filter a lot, export, and are used to SAP; and to see which contracts are busy in the coming weeks.	The most controls on screen. The planning calendar is good for contracts, weaker for “my day”; at phone width it scrolls sideways inside its card.
My recommendation: Week by week

It gives both pages one grammar, so moving between the list and the calendar costs nothing: the same weeks, the same order, the same marks. Late work is on top by itself, this week is open, and everything later is one line you can open, which keeps the page calm without hiding anything (each folded line says what is inside and what it costs).

Who acts is still visible on every row (initials for ours, the other party's letters and a Chase button for theirs, a grey ring for nobody). Jambo's finds sit on today's line as one row with “Review 3”, not as a banner. If chasing is most of your day, Whose move is the better pick.

Checks that changed what I drew
The cheapest channel: Jambo's three finds are a row in the list with “Review 3”, never a strip across the page. The review desk itself is unchanged and opens on the desk.
Jambo's place and cost: “plan my week” says “1 Jambo call” on the button before the press; his blocks are dashed until you press Add; his reading of your week is counted from the list, so it says “no Jambo call”.
One door: Chase is the same act everywhere it is drawn (row, calendar chip, panel) and only on their obligations; on ours it is greyed with the reason. “Decide” on the lease opens the renewal decision on the contract, not a second one here.
Rules kept visible: the private employment agreement wears its lock and shows no money; “Waiting on a step” is never counted as late or chased.
Your three screens: to keep six weeks in the window, each calendar's key sits in its own head row and the month fills whatever height is left. All three calendars fit at 1440×780 and 1366×950. At 1536×730, Whose move fits, Week by week is 6 points short (one hairline), and the SAP way's planning calendar needs a short scroll (about 50 points) because its filter band takes the room. The obligations lists are longer than any window, as today, so they scroll inside the desk; the page head and Jambo's panel stay put.
