A contract's Dossier

Open a contract and its paper stands in the middle, with Jambo's panel beside it. The panel has three tabs: Dossier, Clauses and Redlines. The Dossier is the six stages across the top, then one even list of what to look at and what still waits.

  1. Read the spine: six stages across the top, the current one marked.
  2. Press a line. What it opens comes to the middle, where the paper was; the Dossier never moves. “‹ Paper” or Esc brings the paper back.
  3. Under the list: Obligations, the links, then the stage's two acts at the foot. Every other act is in the paper's More.
  4. On Clauses, each point Jambo found carries its doors as green links. A blank carries only Fill it in.

  3. Words. A subtitle carries only what the list below cannot: the count and the money. A foot line stays only where it states a rule the page acts on. A hint is one short line. Nothing is said twice between a head and Jambo's panel.
  4. Colour. Ink and grey carry the page. HaTi's green marks the live tab, the links and Jambo's box. Amber, red and green stand only for a state, always with a word beside them. Jambo's cards take a faint indigo wash and nothing else is tinted. When any of this is built, the build reads the Design tokens page strictly (owner, 31 Oct 2026): no page invents a colour, a size, a spacing or a type step; a value that is not a token is a defect.
  5. Convenience. A control sits where the hand already is: a row's acts at the row's end, a page's acts under the thing they act on and no wider than it, never spread across the whole width. The main button is last on the right; a hint stands before the buttons it explains, never after. What you will put into the contract is always quoted before you press.
  6. Release, every round. Each round also moves the design toward market release, not only the page that was pointed at: the bad days are drawn (empty, failed, expired, away), icons are drawn not typed, every colour is measured, and the system is one page the code reads. What still falls short is named at the end of each round.
  7. The line slides. On every page the line between the page and Jambo's panel is the handle, as in HaTi today: drag it and the panel follows, it snaps back near 384, a double-press puts it back, the width is kept per page in this browser.

The frame: pages, not cards

One page, one line. HaTi's page and Jambo's panel are one surface; a single hairline divides them, the way Claude's side panel sits beside its main area.

Jambo stops looking like a visitor in a floating box and reads as part of HaTi, always there on the right, with his own head line level with the page's.

What changes

  • No grey ground around the page, no rounded corners, no shadow on the page or on Jambo's panel.
  • The page's top line and Jambo's tab line are the same height, so the line under them runs straight across.
  • The paper keeps its shadow on its desk, and pop-ups keep theirs: those are things lying on top of the page.
  • The clause editor's panel docks to the side instead of floating.

What it gives

At 1440×780 the page gains about 43 px of width and 28 px of height. On a contract the first line of wording moves up 8 px.

My recommendation

The way: The Legora Way, as drawn here. It is the direction you leaned to, and these four screens show it holds across the whole day: what waits, a paper, a negotiation, and a question across the book.

The type: Editorial. Your product is about paper, and Newsreader makes the paper look like one; Inter beside it keeps every list and label readable at the small sizes HaTi uses. It is the pairing where the paper is the hero and the app steps back, which is exactly the Legora Way's idea. If you want HaTi to stay more its own, keep Today: it is a good pairing and nothing is wrong with it.

The frame: Page. It gives the paper more room on your two laptops and the iPad, and it makes Jambo part of the page rather than a box beside it. Keep the shadow only where something really lies on top: the paper on its desk, and pop-ups.
